# Finite strong Steiner categories: census plan

Current record fields and encodings are specified in `RECORD.md`; browser behavior is specified in `TYPEBASE.md` where present. This plan also describes future work. Current release scope is selected in `../VOLUME-I.md`, and checks count as evidence only when an accompanying run report records their execution.

**Status:** identity, light, and mapping enrichment certified through atomic
size 12; rich enrichment and TypeBase publication complete through size 11,
2026-08-28
**Active product:** a certified census of finite strong Steiner strict
`omega`-categories and its TypeBase Volume I catalogue.
**Equivalence relation:** strict isomorphism, categorical equivalence, and folk
equivalence; these agree in the finite strong Steiner class.
**Census size:** total number of atoms, refined by the atomic dimension vector.

This collection is the second concrete test of Noether, TypeLake, and TypeBase.
It should reuse the successful Poset division of responsibilities: Noether owns
reusable mathematics, a collection-specific crate owns enumeration and record
construction, TypeLake accepts immutable validated batches, and TypeBase assigns
stable labels and builds the reader-facing catalogue.

The term **Steiner category** in this plan always means a **finite strong
Steiner strict `omega`-category**. Equivalently, it is a strict
`omega`-category freely generated by a strongly loop-free polygraphic basis.
Its canonical algebraic model is a unital, strongly loop-free augmented
directed complex with a finite basis. We will write `C = nu(K)` for this
correspondence.

This scope choice is deliberate. The weaker classes of arbitrary strict
`omega`-categories, arbitrary free polygraphs, and Steiner complexes that are
loop-free but not strongly loop-free are not part of the first census.

## 1. Decisions at a glance

1. The stored mathematical identity is the canonical finite based augmented
   directed complex `K`, not an expansion of every composite cell of `nu(K)`.
2. Strict isomorphism, categorical equivalence, and folk equivalence agree for
   strong Steiner categories: these categories are gaunt and loop-free, so an
   equivalence has no nonidentity equivalences with which to hide a failure of
   strict bijectivity. Canonicalization computes this common equivalence class
   using the finite strict model. Weak equivalence of classifying spaces remains
   a different relation.
3. The primary census parameter is the **total number of atoms**
   `a = a_0 + ... + a_d`. The exact coverage region also records the atomic
   dimension vector `(a_0, ..., a_d)`.
4. The TypeBase label is `a.d.c`: total atoms, categorical dimension, and the
   one-based position in canonical-key order within that bucket. The object
   namespace makes `SteinerCategory/7.2.3` distinct from `Poset/7.2.3`.
5. Noether gains a ninth public domain, `category`, in Rust and Python. The
   addition is additive; existing eight-domain APIs do not move.
6. Enumeration remains in TypeLake's collection-specific builder, not in
   Noether.
7. Integral homology of the directed complex is published as **polygraphic
   homology**. The reader-facing categorical-homology invariant is the family
   of Gepner--Heine homotopy Posets of
   `Omega^infinity(H(N) ∧ Σ∞_cat C+)`, where the smash product is the
   stabilization of the Gray tensor product. The free stratified simplicial
   commutative monoid on the Street nerve is retained as an exact
   computational source model, not presented as if it were a list of homology
   groups.
8. The main visual is one colored 2-computad plate: every 0-atom and generating
   1-cell occurs once, while each generating 2-cell is an uninterrupted membrane
   or transverse tile spanning its two boundary paths. A conventional
   double-shaft arrow records the 2-cell orientation. Overlapping or nonplanar faces receive
   separate transverse positions within the same plate rather than duplicated
   copies of the category. The restrained primary-color vocabulary follows
   Oliver Byrne's Euclid plates. Selecting two displayed 0-atoms opens the
   exact mapping category between them.
9. Enumerate monomorphic oriented basepoints up to the natural action of the
   category automorphism group. For each one, compute the Gepner--Heine
   homotopy poset and link its canonical `PosetKey` to the finite-Poset
   catalogue whenever that TypeBase volume contains it.
10. Noether implements the non-symmetric Gray tensor product through the tensor
    product of based augmented directed complexes. TypeBase publishes a small
    ordered multiplication table and exact tensor-product relations.
11. There is no Poset-style core: the three relevant equivalence relations have
    already collapsed to strict isomorphism in this class.
12. The “interesting” page combines a curated editorial register with one
    transparent structural rule: include every category having a basepoint
    whose homotopy core has more points than connected components. This admits
    a nontrivial core while excluding arbitrarily large homotopically discrete
    antichains. The same predicate is independently available as a search
    restriction.

## 2. Mathematical model

### 2.1 Defining data

A finite based augmented directed complex consists of finite free abelian
groups

```text
K_d --partial_d--> ... --partial_2--> K_1 --partial_1--> K_0 --epsilon--> Z
```

with a distinguished basis `B_q` for every `K_q`. The positive submonoid is the
free commutative monoid generated by `B_q`. For this census:

- `epsilon(b) = 1` for every `b in B_0`;
- every boundary is stored as
  `partial(b) = partial_plus(b) - partial_minus(b)` with disjoint positive and
  negative supports;
- the basis is unital;
- the precedence relation generated by

  ```text
  a < b when a occurs in partial_minus(b)
  a < b when b occurs in partial_plus(a)
  ```

  is acyclic.

The last condition is strong loop-freeness. A topological ordering is a
validation witness and a search aid; a chosen ordering is not part of the
mathematical object.

For a unital loop-free basis, Steiner proves that every term of every cell of
`nu(K)` is a sum of distinct basis elements. In particular, the boundary
matrices of a strong Steiner complex have entries in `{-1, 0, 1}`, and a finite
basis produces only finitely many cells. This is the key finiteness fact behind
both storage and enumeration.

### 2.2 Atoms, cells, and size

The basis elements are the **atoms** or polygraphic generators. They are not all
the cells: composites and identities are also cells of `nu(K)`. We therefore
keep these counts separate everywhere:

```text
atomic_dimension_vector = (a_0, ..., a_d)
atomic_size             = sum(a_q)
cell_counts             = exact-dimensional nonidentity cell counts
```

The last value is derived by finite cell enumeration and is never used as
identity. An object with one `q`-atom has at least two atoms in every lower
degree: the iterated positive and negative faces are nonempty and disjoint.
Thus a category of dimension `d` has atomic size at least `2d + 1`. Bounding
atomic size automatically bounds dimension, but the dimension vector remains
the useful fine-grained coverage coordinate.

“Through degree `d`” alone would not be a finite census: already in degree 1
there are categories with arbitrarily many objects and arrows. Every
completeness statement must include atomic-size or atomic-vector bounds.

### 2.3 Why the chain model is the right identity

Steiner's `lambda` and `nu` functors restrict to an equivalence between strong
Steiner categories and strong Steiner complexes. On these complexes `nu` is
fully faithful. An isomorphism of based augmented directed complexes preserves
the positive cones in both directions, so it permutes the unique bases degree
by degree. Consequently:

- category isomorphism is exact degree-preserving permutation equivalence of
  the signed boundary data;
- canonicalization does not need to expand composites;
- the natural automorphism group acts faithfully on the finite atom set;
- all category operations can still be checked against exact cell semantics.

Strong Steiner categories are gaunt: every invertible higher cell is an
identity. They are also loop-free in every iterated mapping category. A
categorical or folk equivalence is therefore bijective on objects rather than
merely essentially surjective, and inductively bijective on every mapping
category rather than merely locally equivalent. Hence it is a strict
isomorphism. The implementation still canonicalizes strict finite boundary
data; the theorem says that the resulting key classifies all three named
relations. The induction is stated in `RECORD.md`; exhaustive tiny computations are validation evidence, not the proof.

### 2.4 Complete and enriched regions

The current identity and light-enrichment release contains all isomorphism
classes of atomic size at most 12; rich enrichment remains complete through
size 11. The size-7 pilot supplied the first certification gate and already
contained genuinely higher examples:

- the 2-globe, with vector `(2, 2, 1)`;
- the 2-oriental, with vector `(3, 3, 1)`;
- the 3-globe, with vector `(2, 2, 2, 1)`.

The first genuinely two-variable Gray example, the directed square
`D^1 tensor D^1`, has `3 * 3 = 9` atoms and therefore lies in the complete
release. The accepted shell counts for sizes 0 through 12 are
`1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 8, 17, 50, 137, 451, 1483, 5412, 20444`, totaling 28,010
identities. The first 7,566 categories through size 11 comprise the complete
rich region. The exhaustive signed-boundary oracle agrees with the orderly
generator through size 8; an unpruned recursive attachment oracle agrees
through size 11.

The incidence-graph canonicalizer makes identity-only enumeration much
cheaper than enrichment. On the 2026-08-27 development machine, complete
shells 11, 12, and 13 contained respectively 5,412, 20,444, and 82,919 classes
and took about 3.5, 12.4, and 57.6 seconds to enumerate after warm compilation.
Publishing every mapping category, basepoint, Street skeleton, group
identification, and static detail page grows much faster. We therefore allow a
larger **identity census** and a smaller **fully enriched catalogue**, with
coverage stated per invariant. Atomic size 12 is now certified in both the
identity and light lanes; size 13 is feasible but already calls for sharding
and a data host rather than a monolithic static-site repository.

Curated named examples may lie beyond the complete census region. They are
constructed records and must not be presented as evidence that their whole
size bucket is complete.

### 2.5 Poset-scale identity horizon

TypeBase currently contains 2,769,965 finite Posets through order 10. The
Steiner identity generator has measured 110,929 classes through atomic size
13, of which the 28,010 classes through size 12 are currently certified.
Extrapolating
the measured shell ratios gives a planning range of roughly 340,000--375,000
classes at size 14 and 1.45--1.90 million at size 15, or approximately
1.9--2.4 million classes through size 15. These are capacity estimates, not
certified counts. Completeness through size 15 is therefore the natural target
for a Steiner collection on the same numerical scale as the Poset collection;
size 16 would likely overshoot it substantially.

The present all-enrichments path must not simply be multiplied to that range.
At size 11 the collection already averages about 18 ordered mapping records
and 22 monomorphic-basepoint orbits per category. Applying those ratios to two
million identities would create around 36 million mapping rows and 44 million
basepoint rows. The current one-JSON-detail-file-per-category TypeBase edition
would extrapolate to tens of gigabytes and millions of files.

The scale-up therefore has three explicit lanes:

1. **Complete identity lane through size 15.** Shard canonical-parent
   generation by a deterministic key prefix, checkpoint each shard, and merge
   by external key order. Store the canonical boundary data. The independent
   recursive oracle remains the certification
   floor through size 11; larger shells rely on the proved canonical-parent
   rule plus complete shard accounting.
2. **Uniform light enrichment.** Compute atomic vector, dimension,
   connectedness, polygraphic homology, basic dualities, and the natural
   automorphism order where the canonicalizer already supplies it. Exact cell
   expansion, all mappings, all monomorphic basepoints, Street data, and stable
   particle slices are not prerequisites for admitting an identity.
3. **Mapping and exact-cell enrichment.** Materialize exact nonidentity cells
   and every ordered object-pair mapping category in its own immutable lane.
   This lane is complete through size 12 and can advance independently of
   basepoints, Street data, and stable particle slices.
4. **Rich enrichment region.** Retain complete rich data through the current
   size 11 boundary, consider size 12 after profiling, and enrich larger
   categories either by an editorial/structural predicate or on demand into an
   immutable cache. Every search field states the range in which it is
   complete.

The implemented lane contracts are
`strong-steiner-categories/identities/v1`,
`strong-steiner-categories/light-enrichments/v1`,
`strong-steiner-categories/mapping-enrichments/v1`, and
`strong-steiner-categories/rich-enrichments/v1`. Their immutable shell
directories carry independent run, validation, and per-invariant coverage
manifests. The size-12 identity run accepted 20,444 representatives from
134,304 raw attachments; the complete light and mapping enrichments were then
independently recomputed during validation. The mapping shell contains 391,023
ordered object-pair records and occupies 2.2 MB compressed with its manifests.

The Poset browser already supplies the publication model: one Parquet search
shard per complete shell and bounded Parquet detail parts, queried in the
browser. A Poset-scale Steiner edition should replace millions of individual
detail JSON files with the same arrangement. Labels remain
`atomic-size.dimension.index` and are assigned only after an entire bucket is
merged and frozen.

## 3. Ownership and repository boundary

### 3.1 Noether

Noether owns reusable category mathematics:

- construction and exact validation of finite strong Steiner categories;
- atoms, cells, source, target, identities, and composition;
- the `lambda`/`nu` algebraic realization used by this finite model;
- canonical form, isomorphism, and automorphism group;
- mapping categories and truncations;
- oriented basepoints, their monomorphism predicate, iterated mapping
  categories, and homotopy posets;
- dualities;
- the Gray tensor product and named mathematical constructors such as globes,
  orientals, and cubes;
- the underlying exact chain complex and integral polygraphic homology;
- the Street nerve and Heine categorical-homology source presentation;
- finite particle-number slices and fixed particle sectors of the free
  symmetric-monoidal infinite-loop model for categorical homology.

Noether does not assign TypeBase labels, enumerate a census, write shards,
choose a completeness region, emit TypeLake rows, or lay out an SVG.

### 3.2 TypeLake repository

The collection-specific Rust crate will live in this repository, initially as
`crates/steiner-census/`. It owns:

- the redundant reference generator;
- the hereditary orderly generator;
- canonical-parent construction, sharding, checkpoints, and merge;
- enrichment orchestration;
- automorphism-orbit enumeration of monomorphic basepoints;
- categorical-homology skeleton, homotopy-poset, and Gray-product batches;
- the versioned collection record and validation reports;
- TypeLake batch construction and the TypeBase projection inputs.

It consumes only Noether's public Rust facade. It must not copy category
mathematics into the builder.

### 3.3 TypeLake and TypeBase

TypeLake accepts canonical objects, enrichments, relations, provenance, and
coverage evidence. TypeBase Volume I selects a frozen coherent census, assigns
labels, resolves optional enrichments, and supplies static search/view data.

The legacy SciType packages and global schema registry do not constrain this
collection. The collection owns an ordinary local `RECORD.md`, Rust record
types, JSON interchange, Arrow/Parquet schema, and conformance fixtures.

### 4.1 `SteinerCategory`

`SteinerCategory` is the sole owning object for a finite strong Steiner
category. It stores immutable graded atomic bases and sparse signed boundary
columns. It does not store a second copy as a generic chain complex.

Proposed operations are:

```text
SteinerCategory
  from_boundaries(atom_counts, sparse_boundaries) -> Result<Self, CategoryError>
  empty() -> Self
  globe(dimension) -> Self
  oriental(dimension) -> Self
  cube(dimension) -> Self

  dimension() -> i64
  atom_count() -> usize
  atom_counts() -> &[usize]
  boundary(atom) -> SignedBoundary
  precedence_poset() -> Poset
  verify() -> Result<(), VerificationError>

  atom(atom) -> SteinerCell
  cells() -> Result<Vec<SteinerCell>, CategoryError>
  mapping_category(source_object, target_object)
      -> Result<FiniteOmegaCategory, CategoryError>
  monomorphic_oriented_basepoints(dimension)
      -> Result<Vec<OrientedBasepoint>, CategoryError>
  homotopy_poset(basepoint) -> Result<Poset, CategoryError>
  truncate(dimension) -> Result<Self, CategoryError>

  canonical_label() -> Result<SteinerCanonicalLabel, CategoryError>
  canonical_form() -> Result<Self, CategoryError>
  is_isomorphic(other) -> Result<bool, CategoryError>
  automorphism_group() -> Result<PermutationGroup, CategoryError>

  dual(dimensions) -> Result<Self, CategoryError>
  gray_tensor(other) -> Result<Self, CategoryError>
  underlying_chain_complex() -> ChainComplex<IntegerRing>
  polygraphic_homology()
      -> Result<GradedHomology<IntegerRing>, HomologyError>
  street_nerve_skeleton(max_simplicial_degree)
      -> Result<StreetNerveSkeleton, CategoryError>
  categorical_homology()
      -> CategoricalHomologyPresentation
```

Exact Rust spellings should be settled during the API review, but the
mathematical ownership should not change.

Construction performs all of the following checks:

1. dimensions and sparse-matrix shapes agree;
2. every entry is `-1`, `0`, or `1`;
3. positive and negative supports are disjoint;
4. consecutive boundaries compose to zero;
5. iterated positive and negative 0-boundaries each augment to one;
6. the generated precedence graph is acyclic.

A `SteinerCategory` therefore cannot represent an invalid or merely presumed
Steiner object.

### 4.2 Cells and mapping categories

A cell of `nu(K)` has the finite double-sequence representation

```text
(x_0_minus, x_0_plus | ... | x_d_minus, x_d_plus),
```

where every entry is a subset of the atoms in that degree. `SteinerCell` uses
bitsets and is tied to its category context. It exposes exact source, target,
dimension, identity, and composition operations. Cell enumeration is lazy and
cached; category construction must not expand all composites.

For objects `x` and `y`, `C(x,y)` has as objects the 1-cells from `x` to `y`,
as 1-cells the corresponding 2-cells, and so on. These mapping categories are
Steiner categories. They need not arrive with a verified **strong** Steiner
basis in the representation used by the present collection. Therefore
`mapping_category()` returns an explicit `FiniteOmegaCategory`, not a falsely
strong `SteinerCategory`. In dimension 2 this value is an ordinary finite
category and supports the browser view directly. In higher dimension the same
operation can be applied recursively.

Noether also attempts an exact `strong_steiner_model()` reconstruction. When
one exists, the mapping sidecar stores its canonical `SteinerKey`, and TypeBase
links directly to that category's `a.d.c` label whenever it lies in the
published shell. This is a partial relation into the **strong** Steiner
collection, not a claim that the remaining mapping categories fail to be
Steiner. In the current size-at-most-11 census, 132,742 of
132,915 mapping categories have a verified strong-Steiner model; 173 do not.
Eighty-seven of the verified models have more than 11 atoms, so a mapping category need not have fewer
atoms than its ambient category either. Exact cells are retained in every
case.

### 4.3 Canonical form and symmetry

`SteinerCanonicalLabel` contains:

- the canonical category;
- the degree-preserving permutation from submitted atom ids to canonical ids;
- the complete automorphism group acting on the submitted flattened atom ids;
- exact verification methods.

The canonical labeling is not generally an automorphism, just as for Posets.
Only equal canonical leaves yield automorphisms.

The implementation encodes the signed boundary data as a vertex-coloured,
edge-weighted directed incidence graph. Atom dimension is an immutable colour,
negative and positive boundary incidences have weights `-1` and `+1`, and
dimension-coloured self-loops make degree blocks intrinsic. Noether's exact
graph-symmetry search computes one canonical labelling and the automorphism
group from the same search tree. The induced atom order is then checked by
reconstructing and verifying the canonical strong-Steiner category. This
avoids factorial iteration over whole degree blocks while keeping the result
exact and independent of the submitted atom names.

Before creating a new kernel crate, benchmark this exact caller. The first
implementation belongs in `crates/noether/src/category/`; a separate kernel is
justified only by a demonstrated second caller or a measured bottleneck.

### 4.4 Three distinct homology constructions

Strong Steiner categories are free `omega`-categories, hence cofibrant for the
folk model structure. Their polygraphic homology is computed by the homology of
the linearized chain complex on their atomic basis. Noether routes this through
the existing `homalg::ChainComplex<IntegerRing>` and its integral homology
playbook. The core object row calls this value:

```text
integral_polygraphic_homology
```

and never merely `homology`.

Hadrian Heine's categorical homology is not another graded list of abelian
groups. The object we want readers to compare is the infinite-loop category of
the categorical spectrum

```text
Omega^infinity H_N(C) = Omega^infinity(H(N) ∧ Σ∞_cat C+),
```

where `Σ∞_cat` is categorical stabilization and the smash product is the
stabilized Gray product. Its finite, order-valued invariants are the
Gepner--Heine homotopy Posets

```text
hPos_(q,z)(Omega^infinity H_N(C)).
```

Heine's categorical Dold--Thom theorem identifies this infinite-loop category
with the free strict symmetric-monoidal infinity-category on `C`. This is an
exact stable identification, not a finite-level approximation requiring an
empirical stable range.

The zeroth-space/source presentation is modeled combinatorially by

```text
N[N(C)]
```

the free stratified simplicial commutative monoid on the Street nerve `N(C)`.
For a finite category, every fixed simplicial degree is finite, while the whole
categorical-homology object and each free commutative monoid generally are not.
We therefore store an exact lazy `CategoricalHomologyPresentation` linked to
the source `SteinerKey`, plus a certified finite `StreetNerveSkeleton`:

- degree `r` generators are the functors from the `r`th oriental to `C`;
- using Steiner's fully faithful chain model, enumerate those functors as
  augmentation-preserving positive chain maps from the simplex chain complex
  `C(Delta^r)` to the directed complex of `C`, extending dimension by
  dimension and rejecting a partial map as soon as a boundary equation fails;
- face, degeneracy, and thin-marking maps are explicit;
- the free commutative monoid is stored by its finite generator presentation,
  never by enumerating all of its elements;
- `complete_through_simplicial_degree` says exactly how far the materialized
  presentation goes, while the construction recipe defines every later degree;
- the current materialization is a uniform degree 2 skeleton.

The chain-map route is independently checked against literal functor
enumeration for the tiny fixtures. Unlike basepoint storage, Street-nerve
simplices are not quotiented by `Aut(C)`: each simplex is a generator of the
free commutative monoid. Automorphisms may organize or accelerate enumeration,
but orbit counts cannot replace the full presentation.

The non-reduced presentation is attached once to `C` and remains source
evidence for the stable calculation. Its zeroth homotopy Poset is generally
infinite, but is canonically graded by particle number. The weight-`r` slice is
the finite symmetric power of `pi_0(C)`. For fixed source and target words, the
fundamental Poset is the finite disjoint union over particle permutations of
Cartesian products of source fundamental Posets. Fixed higher sectors are
Cartesian products of source homotopy Posets. The current census materializes
the zeroth slices through weight 2 and stores the one-particle higher Posets;
the product/permutation presentation defines every other finite sector.

Ordinary integral homology of the Street nerve is a third invariant:

```text
integral_street_nerve_homology
```

It may be computed from a sufficiently complete normalized nerve chain
complex as a separately versioned enrichment. Polygraphic and Street-nerve
homology may be identified only when a recorded theorem or computation proves
homological coherence for that object or family. Categorical homology is never
collapsed to either graded abelian-group invariant.

### 4.5 Monomorphic basepoints and homotopy posets

An oriented basepoint of dimension `r` is a functor

```text
partial D^(r + 1) -> C,
```

equivalently a sequence of pairs `(X_i, Y_i)` for `0 <= i <= r`, where
`X_i` and `Y_i` are parallel `i`-cells from `X_(i-1)` to `Y_(i-1)`.
The database restricts to **monomorphic** basepoints: the displayed boundary
functor must be a monomorphism in the categorical sense, equivalently an
embedding on the maximal subspace and recursively on mapping spaces. In this
finite gaunt strict setting, the reference implementation checks injectivity on
every cell of the explicit globe boundary. The expected recursive fast test is
that the two chosen cells are distinct at every level; Noether uses this strict-cell test in its enumeration. `RECORD.md` states its injectivity argument and the scope of the claim.

Noether's `homotopy_poset()` accepts any valid oriented basepoint. The census
enumeration deliberately omits nonmonomorphic and degenerate boundaries such
as `(A,A)`; those remain on-demand computations rather than silently entering
the monomorphic-basepoint table.

For a dimension `n - 1` basepoint `Z`, Gepner--Heine define

```text
pi_n(C, Z) = pi_0(Mor_C^n(Z)).
```

Also compute the unbased zeroth homotopy Poset `pi_0(C)` once: its elements are
the objects of `C`, ordered by existence of a 1-cell. It uses the same Poset
linking pipeline as the based values below.

Noether constructs the iterated mapping category exactly. Its objects are the
`n`-cells filling the selected boundary, and one object is below another when
there is a higher cell between them. The iterated mapping category remains
gaunt and loop-free, so reachability is already antisymmetric. Transitive
closure therefore gives a finite Poset, which is passed unchanged to
`combin::Poset::canonical_label()`.

The result stores the canonical `PosetKey` regardless of size. If that key is
present in the selected finite-Poset TypeBase volume, the page links to its
stable `Poset/a.b.c` label and inherits the Poset page's core, homology, and
SVG. A result outside the complete Poset range remains an exact constructed
Poset object with no invented catalogue label. Equal Posets arising from
different basepoints are deduplicated, while every occurrence relation is
retained.

The fundamental case has a useful independent implementation: for objects
`A,B`, enumerate paths of atomic 1-cells from `A` to `B` and order them through
the atomic 2-cell relation described by Gepner--Heine. Compare that result with
the generic `pi_0(C(A,B))` construction for every census object. It is an
oracle and possible fast path, not a second definition.

### 4.6 Gray tensor product

The Gray tensor product is computed on the owning algebraic models. If `a` is
a degree `p` atom and `b` a degree `q` atom, `a tensor b` is a degree `p + q`
atom and

```text
partial(a tensor b)
  = partial(a) tensor b + (-1)^p a tensor partial(b).
```

The paired basis makes the tensor again a finite strong Steiner complex. Thus:

```text
atomic_vector(C tensor D)_r = sum_(p+q=r) a_p(C) a_q(D)
atomic_size(C tensor D)     = atomic_size(C) * atomic_size(D)
dimension(C tensor D)       = dimension(C) + dimension(D).
```

`gray_tensor()` validates the tensor boundary, canonicalizes the result, and
returns a normal `SteinerCategory`. The product is non-symmetric, so relation
records and the public table use ordered pairs; `C tensor D` and `D tensor C`
are both computed rather than identified. The one-point category is the unit,
the empty category is absorbing, and `D^1 tensor D^1` is the directed square.
The displayed dimension formula is for nonempty factors; the explicit empty
convention handles the zero complex.

The first multiplication page has as rows and columns every canonical category
of atomic size at most 3:

```text
empty, D^0, the discrete two-point category,
the discrete three-point category, D^1
```

This is a `5 x 5` table, its products have at most 9 atoms, and it already
contains the directed square `D^1 tensor D^1`. It displays the canonical
category label when the result lies in a certified shell, otherwise a clearly
marked construction page. This fixed 5-by-5 table is the implemented scope.
A larger ordered subtable is future work and is not a completeness claim of
this edition.

### 4.7 Dualities

Strict higher categories have more than one reversal. The general operation is
indexed by the set of cell dimensions to reverse. For 2-categories the browser
uses the familiar names:

- `op`: reverse 1-cells;
- `co`: reverse 2-cells;
- `coop`: reverse both.

The exact mask convention and its action on the directed complex must be
documented and tested before the record is frozen. TypeLake stores one
`duality` relation with a dimension-mask payload. `is_self_dual(mask)` is a
derived equality with the canonical target. We do not create several
unqualified `dual` and `opposite` fields with overlapping meanings.

- the 2-oriental;
- the directed square/2-cube;
- disjoint unions;
- invalid loop, non-complex, non-unital, and cyclic-precedence inputs;
- canonicalization under every degree-preserving relabeling of small cases;
- exact composition and mapping categories for the 2-oriental;
- monomorphic basepoints and homotopy Posets for arrows, orientals, and cubes;
- the fundamental-poset fast path against generic iterated mapping categories;
- Street-nerve skeletons and categorical-homology presentations for globes and
  orientals;
- Gray unit laws, the directed square, the Koszul boundary sign, and products
  in both factor orders.

## 5. Canonical key and identity

### 5.1 Labeled encoding

For each degree `q`, label its atoms `0, ..., a_q - 1`. For each positive-degree
atom, record two bitsets in degree `q - 1`:

```text
negative_boundary_support
positive_boundary_support
```

Their signed difference is the corresponding boundary-matrix column.
The implemented `SteinerKey` is the signed-incidence-graph canonical encoding
described in `RECORD.md`: the word begins with the number of occupied degrees,
and canonical bytes encode its `u64` words big-endian. It is a relation key,
not a cryptographic TypeLake identifier. Records do not carry a separate
canonical-id hash.

### 5.2 Canonicalization

Noether canonically labels the signed incidence graph, with degree and sign
colors, and reconstructs the canonical category from that labeling. It does
not minimize a separately declared little-endian encoding over permutations.

### 5.3 Automorphisms

The natural action is on the flattened atom set, ordered in degree blocks. It
is usually intransitive and always preserves degree. Store:

- exact verified generators;
- group order;
- orbits, with each orbit contained in one degree;
- optional abstract-group identification with GAP `SmallGroup` and LMFDB
  labels when available.

As with Posets, there is no transitive-action label. The category's natural
action, not an abstract isomorphic group, is the primary symmetry value.

## 6. Enumeration

Noether supplies mathematical predicates and canonicalization. The census
crate supplies two generators whose overlap is compared exactly.

### 6.1 Reference matrix generator

For every atomic dimension vector in a small shell:

1. set the augmentation of every 0-atom to one;
2. enumerate disjoint negative/positive subset pairs for every boundary
   column, hence all ternary `{-1,0,1}` columns of the required shape;
3. reject as soon as `partial^2 != 0`;
4. check unitality by recursively taking signed positive and negative parts;
5. build the precedence graph and reject cycles;
6. canonicalize and deduplicate by `SteinerKey`.

This algorithm is intentionally redundant and simple. It is the hand-auditable
oracle for low atomic sizes, not the production route.

### 6.2 Cell-attachment generator

The production generator is hereditary. Starting from a canonical lower
skeleton, adjoin one atom of the largest occupied dimension or of the next
dimension.

For a proposed `q`-atom:

1. enumerate the `(q - 1)`-cells of the lower skeleton by their bitset
   representations;
2. choose a pair of parallel cells with disjoint top atomic supports;
3. use their top supports as the negative and positive boundary of the new
   atom;
4. reject if the extended precedence graph is cyclic;
5. canonicalize the child;
6. keep it only when deleting its canonically distinguished top-dimensional
   atom returns the canonical parent.

Atoms in the same top degree do not occur in one another's boundaries, but
their source/target precedence constraints can interact. The acyclicity check
therefore remains global after every attachment.

### 6.3 Orbit pruning and canonical parent

The automorphism group of the parent acts on candidate boundary pairs. Generate
one representative per orbit before invoking full child canonicalization.
Cache the parent's finite cell table, parallelism classes, and boundary-pair
orbits.

The parent of a non-discrete object deletes the canonically last atom in its
largest occupied degree. No higher atom can refer to it, so the result remains
a based subcomplex and stays strong Steiner. The parent of a discrete
0-category deletes its canonical last object. This gives a unique path down in
atomic size and permits deterministic shards by parent-key prefix.

The implementation must include a written proof that this parent rule is
isomorphism invariant and that every nonempty object is reachable. Tests are
not a replacement for that proof.

### 6.4 Dimension-1 specialization as an oracle

In dimension 1, a finite strong Steiner category is the free category on a
finite directed acyclic multigraph. Parallel generating arrows are allowed;
loops and directed cycles are not. An independent DAG-multigraph generator
will validate every 1-dimensional bucket and provide early benchmark data for
canonicalization and automorphisms.

### 6.5 Enumeration of monomorphic basepoints

Noether enumerates all distinct parallel-cell prefixes. TypeLake then
partitions these explicit basepoints into orbits by iterating the full
automorphism group for each new orbit. Stabilizer-recursive enumeration is a
possible optimization; the current implementation does not use it. Composite
cells and their full whiskering supports participate in the enumeration.
The strict-cell injectivity argument is recorded in `RECORD.md`.

### 6.6 Gray-product table enumeration

Tensor construction does not require a new category census generator. For an
ordered pair of canonical factors, compute the chain-level tensor, verify its
strong Steiner conditions, canonicalize it, and look up its `SteinerKey` in
the frozen label map. Cache by the ordered pair of factor keys. Atom-size
multiplication rejects pairs outside a desired complete subtable before any
boundary work.

For every complete shell `<= A`, enumerate all ordered census pairs satisfying
`atomic_size(C) * atomic_size(D) <= A`. Every result must occur in the same
complete census. Separately compute the fixed factor-size-at-most-3 `5 x 5`
table even when a result lies beyond `A`; those cells are construction records,
not evidence of shell completeness. Verify the atom-vector convolution, Gray
unit laws, the empty absorbing law, and the two factor orders independently.

### 6.7 Sharding and checkpoints

Production shells use the same operational lessons as Posets:

- deterministic canonical-parent ranges;
- bounded-memory sorted parts;
- content-hashed checkpoints;
- restart at a completed part boundary;
- k-way merge with duplicate and parent validation;
- byte-identical output between monolithic, sharded, and resumed runs.

Bulk output requires an explicit `STEINER_CENSUS_DATA_ROOT`. Work files and
logs are replaceable and never become TypeLake merely because a process wrote
them.

### 6.8 Benchmark gates

Before declaring a release cutoff, benchmark by atomic vector, not only total
size:

- number of lower cells and parallel-cell pairs;
- candidate attachments before and after automorphism orbit pruning;
- canonical-label time and nodes visited;
- automorphism group time;
- cell-table and mapping-category expansion;
- monomorphic-basepoint candidates, stabilizer-orbit representatives, and
  homotopy-Poset sizes by degree;
- polygraphic homology time;
- Street-nerve simplex counts and categorical-homology presentation time by
  simplicial degree;
- Gray tensor construction and canonicalization by factor vector;
- peak memory and bytes per accepted record.

Profile highly symmetric globes, parallel-arrow families, long 1-dimensional
chains, wide DAGs, orientals, cubes, and asymmetric random accepted objects.
The likely bottleneck is candidate cell-pair growth or canonical search, not
Smith reduction. The data decides whether size 8, 9, or 10 is the first release
limit.

## 7. Invariants

### 7.1 Required defining and structural values

- canonical `SteinerKey` and boundary support pairs;
- atomic size, dimension, and atomic dimension vector;
- number of objects and generating 1-arrows;
- total signed-boundary nonzeros and boundary mass by degree;
- precedence-poset height, width, and underlying connected-component count;
- connected-component sizes of the undirected generating 1-skeleton;
- exact-dimensional nonidentity cell counts;
- number of mapping-category objects and cells for every ordered object pair;
- flags for discrete, connected, and 1-dimensional categories.

“Connected” means connected in the underlying undirected generating

graph.

### 7.2 Symmetry and duality

- exact automorphism group on atoms, order, generators, and degreewise orbits;
- optional abstract-group labels and validation source;
- canonical target for every supported duality mask;
- self-duality flags;
- orbit counts on objects, generating arrows, and top-dimensional atoms.

### 7.3 Algebraic and categorical invariants

- integral polygraphic homology, including torsion, in every degree;
- polygraphic Euler characteristic, both from atom counts and homology;
- categorical-homology spectrum construction identifier
  `H(N) ∧ Σ∞_cat C+`;
- exact particle grading and Hasse data for every materialized categorical-
  homology component Poset, with a canonical `PosetKey` whenever its order is
  in the current Poset edition;
- exact product/permutation rule reducing fixed higher particle sectors to
  one-particle homotopy Posets;
- exact source-presentation identifier and materialized Street-nerve degree;
- Street-nerve simplex, nondegenerate-simplex, and thin-simplex counts through
  that degree;
- number of 1-cells between every ordered object pair;
- emptiness, discreteness, and connected-component counts of every mapping
  category;
- numbers of initial and terminal objects in the 1-truncation;
- longest directed path in the generating 1-skeleton;
- number of composite versus atomic 1-cells;
- maximum cell count among mapping categories.

### 7.4 Oriented homotopy data

- number of monomorphic oriented basepoints in every dimension, both raw and
  modulo `Aut(C)`;
- orbit-size and stabilizer-order distributions;
- homotopy-Poset orders, dimensions, widths, and empty/singleton flags;
- canonical `PosetKey` and resolved finite-Poset TypeBase link when available;
- multiplicities of each distinct homotopy Poset among basepoint orbits and
  among unquotiented basepoints;
- maximum and total homotopy-Poset order by homotopy degree.

### 7.5 Recognized families and later enrichments

Exact recognizers or construction relations may mark:

- globes;
- orientals;
- cubes;
- simple `omega`-categories in `Theta`;
- suspensions and joins when those operations exist in Noether;
- Gray tensor factors and products.

Useful later enrichments include ordinary Street-nerve homology, homological
coherence evidence, rewriting/confluence properties, indecomposable Gray-tensor
or join decompositions, and counts of functors to or from small named
categories. An exploratory statistic stays out of the durable record until its
definition, value shape, computation, and validation are explicit.

## 8. Worked object: the 2-oriental

The second oriental is the first ideal browser fixture. In one convenient
noncanonical notation its atoms are:

```text
degree 0: v0, v1, v2
degree 1: e01, e02, e12
degree 2: f012
```

with boundaries

```text
partial(e01)  = v1 - v0
partial(e02)  = v2 - v0
partial(e12)  = v2 - v1
partial(f012) = e01 + e12 - e02.
```

Thus `f012` is a 2-cell from the long edge `e02` to the composite
`e12 #_0 e01` under this sign convention. The category has atomic vector
`(3, 3, 1)`, atomic size 7, dimension 2, four nonidentity 1-cells, and one
nonidentity 2-cell. Its mapping category from `v0` to `v2` is the walking arrow:
its two objects are `e02` and `e12 #_0 e01`, and `f012` is the nonidentity
morphism between them.

## 10. TypeBase labels and catalogue organization

The isomorphism-catalogue label is:

```text
a.d.c
```

where:

- `a` is total atomic size;
- `d` is the largest degree containing an atom, with the empty category assigned
  dimension `-1` internally and handled by an explicit catalogue convention;
- `c` is one-based canonical-key order within `(a,d)`.

The empty category has the frozen label `0.-1.1`, using atomic size zero and
categorical dimension `-1`, exactly as in the Poset catalogue convention.

The full dimension vector is searchable but is not embedded in the label. A
variable-length label such as `3.3.1.27` would be harder to read, sort, and cite,
while adding no information the record cannot return immediately.

Stable paths are:

```text
/SteinerCategory/7.2.3
/SteinerCategory/7.2.3/basepoint/1.4
/SteinerCategory/interesting
/SteinerCategory/gray-table
/SteinerCategory/search?...filters...
```

Labels are frozen per TypeBase volume. A later, larger census does not renumber
an existing volume; it may publish a new volume with its own pinned label map.
The category accession names the common strict-isomorphism, categorical-
equivalence, and folk-equivalence class. Basepoint suffixes are frozen only
after every monomorphic basepoint orbit of that source category and degree has
been enumerated.

## 11. Static browser and 2-dimensional view

The browser remains small, static, and museum-catalogue-like. It uses the
existing TypeBase visual language rather than a separate application shell.

### 11.1 Catalogue and search

Facets include:

- atomic size, dimension, and exact atomic vector;
- object, generating-arrow, and total cell counts;
- connectedness and mapping-category size;
- automorphism-group order and LMFDB abstract-group label;
- integral polygraphic Betti numbers and torsion;
- Street-nerve generator counts and categorical-homology materialization
  degree;
- monomorphic-basepoint orbit counts and homotopy-Poset order ranges by degree;
- occurrence of a selected finite Poset as a homotopy Poset;
- self-duality mask;
- named family;
- certified coverage region.

Search results show the label, atomic vector, a compact 2-skeleton thumbnail,
polygraphic homology, symmetry order, and family tags. Completeness badges name
the exact atomic shell or vector region they certify.

### 11.2 Main object view

The default picture is one packed 2-computad plate rather than a repeated face
atlas:

- every 0-atom appears exactly once as an object node;
- every generating 1-atom appears exactly once as a directed arrow, layered
  left-to-right by the acyclic precedence data;
- parallel arrows use stable separated lanes constrained by their 2-cell
  incidence;
- every generating 2-atom is a transverse directed tile between its actual
  negative and positive boundary paths, with its exact path formula in the
  register below the drawing;
- all higher atoms remain in the exact boundary ladder rather than being
  falsely projected into the 2-dimensional plate.

The overview does not pretend that every finite 2-computad has a planar
embedding with disjoint faces. Overlapping and nonplanar 2-cells occupy distinct
transverse positions on their shared paths while objects and arrows remain
singletons. Edge routes must avoid object glyphs and use the collision checks
already learned from the Poset Hasse viewer.

The SVG is available in ten shared presentation plates also used by the Poset
Hasse viewer: Byrne, cyanotype, copperplate, Bauhaus, survey, manuscript,
oscilloscope, tabular ink, cartographic, and chalk. A record-local stepper keeps
the accession open while the user compares plates. These are derived display
choices encoded by the `plate` URL parameter; they do not alter layout data,
records, labels, or validation evidence. The surrounding browser has a separate
25-proof compact presentation room encoded by `style`.

### 11.3 Mapping-category interaction

Clicking one object and then another opens `C(x,y)`:

- in a 2-category, nodes are all 1-cells `x -> y` and arrows are all
  nonidentity 2-cells between them;
- atomic versus composite cells are visibly distinguished;
- selecting a mapping arrow shows its expression in ambient atoms;
- empty homs and identity-only homs receive intentional compact states;
- in dimension greater than 2, the panel becomes a recursive
  `(d - 1)`-category browser.

The mathematics and exact cell table are produced by Noether during the
TypeBase build. When the finite mapping category has a verified strong-Steiner
model, the panel gives its TypeBase label as a live link. If the model is
outside the published atomic range, it reports its atomic size; otherwise the
panel states only that no strong-Steiner presentation is attached. It does not
misdescribe the mapping category as non-Steiner.
Browser TypeScript performs layout, filtering, and selection; it does not
reimplement higher-category composition.

The accepted interaction extension has three parts:

1. mapping categories are primary navigable views, with permanent links when
   their strong-Steiner model has a published label;
2. a generating 2-cell is displayed between its complete source and target
   composite paths, not as though it enters or leaves an intermediate object;
3. the cell register will expose vertical composition, horizontal composition,
   and left and right whiskering through exact Noether operations.

Explicit interchange-witness or pasting-decomposition displays are not part of
this extension.

The main plate is named precisely: it displays `iota_2(C)`, the
2-dimensional truncation of the Steiner category.  In particular, a generating
2-cell whose source or target is a composite is drawn in the interior between
the two whole boundary paths; its direction glyph must never be attached to an
intermediate object.  The record also has a dimension-by-dimension register of
every exact nonidentity cell.  Immediate globular source and target links make
3-cells visible as maps between 2-cells, and continue in higher dimensions.
Iterated identities are determined uniquely and are not redundantly listed in
every higher degree.

Mapping categories and their exact cells form a natural independently
versioned enrichment lane between light and rich data.  They must not be added
retroactively to `light-version=1`: completed layers are immutable, and the
mapping output has a very different row-growth profile.  On the size-12 shell,
20,444 identities produce 391,023 ordered mapping records and 363,715 stored
mapping cells in about 59 seconds on the reference host.  This makes complete
mapping data through size 13 comfortable; size 14 remains a batch computation,
while a browser release at that scale needs sharded columnar search and detail
artifacts.

### 11.3.1 Identifying a supplied example

The static catalogue supports exact lookup by label and, in the next
projection version, by canonical `SteinerKey`.  Arbitrary examples are supplied
as the same signed-boundary JSON accepted by Noether.  Identification is a
bounded Noether operation: validate the directed complex, canonicalize it, and
look up the resulting key.  Browser TypeScript must not reproduce the
canonical-label algorithm.  A deployment may provide this as a small optional
identify endpoint; browsing and all persistent record pages remain static.

Named syntax such as `oriental(2)`, `globe(3)`, `cube(2)`, and `discrete(4)` is
resolved directly to the stored recognized-family fields and does not require
the identify endpoint.

### 11.4 Basepoint and homotopy-Poset browser

The object page has a dedicated **Homotopy Posets** register. Choose homotopy
degree `n`, then an `Aut(C)`-orbit of monomorphic basepoints. The panel shows:

- the nested boundary ladder `(X_0,Y_0),...,(X_(n-1),Y_(n-1))` in the
  2-skeleton and mapping-category views;
- the orbit size and stabilizer;
- the iterated mapping category `Mor_C^n(Z)`;
- a small Hasse diagram of `pi_n(C,Z)`;
- a live link to its `Poset/a.b.c` page when present, including that page's
  homology and core data;
- an exact `PosetKey` download and a constructed-object page when no frozen
  Poset label exists.

The register defaults to orbit representatives; a count switch reports the
number of actual basepoints represented without rendering arbitrary labeled
duplicates. A reverse index on each Poset page lists Steiner categories and
basepoint orbits for which it occurs as a homotopy Poset.

### 11.5 Categorical-homology view

Present polygraphic homology and categorical homology side by side with visibly
different value shapes. The categorical panel leads with
`hPos_(q,z)(Omega^infinity(H(N) ∧ Σ∞_cat C+))`: a particle-graded register
with a live Poset accession when the result belongs to the selected Poset
volume. It states that fixed higher sectors are Cartesian products of the
one-particle basepoint Posets and that the full fundamental Poset is the
disjoint union over particle permutations.

The exact `N[N_Street(C)]` skeleton, generator counts, thin markings, and
operators move under a “computational source” disclosure. The UI must not
render categorical homology as Betti numbers or mistake the source
presentation for the displayed invariant. Ordinary Street-nerve homology,
when later available, gets a third separately titled panel and explicit
coherence comparison evidence.

### 11.6 Gray multiplication table

The Gray page is a compact ordered table. Row is the left factor, column is the
right factor, and a cell gives the result's label, atomic vector, and a tiny
2-skeleton. Both orders remain visible because Gray tensor is not symmetric.
Clicking a cell opens the result and highlights its paired atoms; unclassified
named products carry a construction badge instead of a provisional label. A
coverage note distinguishes the complete bounded census subtable from the
fixed factor-size-at-most-3 table.

### 11.7 Detail page

Alongside the picture, show:

- atomic boundary table and readable source/target expressions;
- atomic vector and exact cell counts;
- mapping-category matrix by ordered object pair;
- polygraphic homology with an explicit convention note;

sum over [C] of (product_q a_q!) / |Aut(C)|

### 13.3 Mathematical family checks

- globes have atomic vector `(2, ..., 2, 1)` and total size `2d + 1`;

  hence vector `(binomial(n+1,1), ..., binomial(n+1,n+1))`;

  Heine's models through the materialized degree;

- dualities preserve atomic vector, automorphism order, and polygraphic
